# Researching for the brief

Read this when starting the domain scan or any search. The method
(research, ask or know; search recipes; source quality; citing and
labels; safety; offline; staleness) is in `../../../references/research.md`:
read it too. This file holds what is specific to the brief.

## Budget (soft)

- Domain scan: about 6 searches, before the questions.
- Research pass: about 8 searches and 4 fetches.
- At most 2 follow-ups per topic.
- Update mode: about 4 searches.
- Overrunning needs a stated reason.

## Topic checklist

| Topic | Feeds | Preferred sources |
|---|---|---|
| Market and competitors | Problem and context, Goals, Success metrics | Analyst reports, news, competitor sites (their claims are marketing) |
| Domain terms | Glossary, Business rules | Industry bodies, standards glossaries, regulator texts |
| Regulation by country | NFRs and compliance, Risks, Constraints | Law text, regulator, official gazette |
| Standards (WCAG, PCI DSS, ISO, RFCs) | NFRs, Architecture drivers | The standards body |
| Existing solutions and prior art | Problem and context, Capabilities | Vendor docs, open-source repos, case studies |
| Technical feasibility | Constraints, Integrations, Risks | Vendor docs and limits, API references |
| Pricing and usage numbers | Architecture drivers, Constraints | Vendor pricing pages, public statistics |

## Where it fits

1. After the intake filter: the domain scan, then the (at most 4)
   questions.
2. Before writing: the research pass over the open facts.
3. The brief's Sources section sits just before Challenges and decisions and Open questions; the
   *Needs verification* list goes in Open questions.
